Controlled laser-power sweep on part004 - methodology + results
================================================================
CORRECTED RERUN (2026-07-31, later) - the previous version of this report
described results from a run affected by a real bug: run_wall.py's
--toolpath flag was used as-is relative to the working directory and was
never joined with --input-data-dir, so every run in the original sweep
silently loaded examples/wall/toolpath.crs (the wall demo's own 38-second
toolpath) instead of part004's real ~800-second toolpath. Verified directly:
the original runs showed peak temperature occurring once at t~15s then
monotonically cooling for the rest of the window, with the hottest node
frozen in a ~2mm patch - physically inconsistent with a real, continuously
active raster. Fixed in run_wall.py (--toolpath now joined with
--input-data-dir). This report reflects the RERUN with the fix. The
original (invalid) analysis outputs are kept, unmodified, at
../power_sweep/analysis_OLD_INVALID/ for comparison/transparency - do not
use them for anything except illustrating the bug's effect.

Dataset: part004_LP400_SSp8_H1.12_SSt1_LH1.0 (raster toolpath, notched
block; the real toolpath was later found to be a genuine dense raster - 17
tracks/layer at exactly 1.12mm spacing - not the sparse raster an earlier
report claimed).
Sweep parameter: *GAUSS_LASER power (field 0), isolation-verified by
generate_parameter_sweep.py (every generated deck diffed line-by-line
against the base; all 10 decks confirmed to differ from base in exactly
one line). Beam radius (1.12) and absorptivity (0.4) held fixed.
Everything else identical by construction: geometry, mesh, toolpath.crs,
composition deck (only the *GAUSS_LASER token differs), --stop-fraction 0.15,
--n-frames 40, materials, boundary conditions.

Sweep values: 400, 600, 800, 1000, 1200 W (5 points, 200W steps).
10 runs total (5 powers x {arc_length, global_x} composition mode), each
completed in ~5.3 minutes with the CORRECT toolpath (vs. ~2 minutes for the
broken runs - the real, continuously active raster does genuinely more
numerical work than a heat source frozen for 80% of the window).

These numbers are dramatically different from the invalid original sweep
(peak temps 1073-2057K there vs. 2629-5599K now; transient max|dT| 24-27K
there vs. 219-490K now - roughly 10-20x larger). The corrected picture:

1. Peak temperature: monotonically increasing, roughly linear-to-mildly-
   accelerating (increments of 738K, 738K, 789K, 758K, 685K per 200W step -
   fairly steady, no strong saturation over this range once the real
   toolpath's full, continuous heating is captured).

2. Melt-pool proxy (% > liquidus): melting is present at EVERY power level
   now (0.001-0.005%), unlike the original sweep where it only began at
   1000W. The real, continuously-active raster reaches melting even at
   400W. Melt fraction grows roughly monotonically with power (with 1000W
   slightly below 1200W's trend but both higher than 800W).

3. Composition-induced transient max|dT|: substantial and clearly power-
   dependent (219K -> 490K from 400W to 1000W), NOT flat as the invalid
   sweep wrongly showed. Quadratic fit R^2=0.905 vs linear R^2=0.830 - both
   reasonably good fits, mildly nonlinear. Notably NON-MONOTONIC: it peaks
   at 1000W (490K) then DROPS at 1200W (432K) - the automated per-interval
   slope check flags [800,1000] as the steepest interval, not [1000,1200].
   This dip is a single data point on a 5-point sweep and should not be
   over-interpreted as a real physical reversal without a repeat run at
   1200W to check it isn't noise - flagged as the top follow-up.

4. Relative composition sensitivity (transient max|dT| / peak temperature
   rise above 300K): 0.094, 0.099, 0.094, 0.106, 0.082 - notably STABLE
   (roughly 0.08-0.11) across the whole range, in contrast to the invalid
   sweep's reported steadily-decreasing trend (0.032 -> 0.015). With the
   correct toolpath, composition choice's relative importance does NOT
   meaningfully change with power over this range (aside from the same
   1200W dip noted above) - the absolute difference grows roughly in step
   with the overall thermal magnitude, not disproportionately faster or
   slower.

5. Final-frame max|dT|: substantial (149-432K) and NOT decayed by the end
   of the 15%-depth window at any power level - a real, qualitative
   difference from the invalid sweep's near-zero (0.004-0.012K) final-frame
   values. This makes physical sense: with real, continuous heating
   throughout the whole window, there is always recent thermal activity
   that hasn't fully re-equilibrated by a fixed depth cutoff, unlike the
   broken runs which had ~80 seconds of pure passive cooling to flatten out
   any residual difference.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
Verdict on linear/nonlinear/threshold (task 5, corrected)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
NONLINEAR (quadratic R^2=0.905 vs linear R^2=0.830) but WITHOUT a clean
single threshold/knee the way the (invalid) original sweep appeared to
show - melting is present at every power level here, so there is no clean
"before/after melting" transition within this range to point to. The
non-monotonic dip at 1200W is the most notable feature and needs a repeat
run to confirm before drawing a firm conclusion about the shape of the
curve at the top of this range.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
Corrected headline finding
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Laser power DOES have a substantial effect on composition-induced thermal
sensitivity over this range (219K to 490K, over 2x) - the opposite of the
original (invalid) sweep's "power has only a weak effect" conclusion. What
stays comparatively stable is the RELATIVE sensitivity (~0.08-0.11 of the
peak temperature rise), suggesting the absolute effect scales roughly in
proportion with the overall thermal magnitude rather than power
disproportionately amplifying or damping composition's importance, over
this specific power range.

ADDENDUM (2026-08-01): explanation for the 1200W non-monotonic dip
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Investigated using already-computed frames (no new simulation - a repeat
run of a deterministic solver would not have told us anything new, since
identical inputs produce identical outputs). Checked, for every power
level, whether the recorded transient max|dT| occurred mid-run (a complete
rise-then-decline cycle was captured within the 119s/15%-depth window) or
right at the final frame (the event was still rising when the window
closed, i.e. the TRUE peak was not captured):

  LP400:  peaked at t=23.8s,  then declined  -> complete cycle captured
  LP600:  peaked at t=65.5s,  then declined  -> complete cycle captured
  LP800:  STILL RISING at t=119.1s (final frame) -> true peak NOT captured
  LP1000: peaked at t=44.6s,  then declined  -> complete cycle captured
  LP1200: STILL RISING at t=119.1s (final frame) -> true peak NOT captured

This is very likely the explanation for the apparent dip at 1200W: 800W and
1200W are the only two sweep values whose composition-driven divergence
event had not finished rising by the fixed 15%-depth cutoff, so their
recorded "transient max" is a lower bound on the true peak, not the true
peak itself. 1000W's recorded value (490K) is a genuine, complete peak;
1200W's (432K) is a truncated, still-rising value that may well exceed
490K if the run continued further. Under this explanation, the dip is a
measurement-window artifact, not a real reversal in the underlying power-
sensitivity relationship - the true relationship may be monotonically
increasing throughout, consistent with the general trend seen in 400/600/
1000W's complete-cycle values.

This is a real methodological finding worth stating plainly: transient
max|dT| computed over a FIXED stop-fraction window is only a reliable
comparison metric across sweep values when every value's event completes
within that window. It did not for 2 of 5 values here. A future rerun
extending 800W and 1200W (or all values, for consistency) to a longer
stop-fraction - not a repeat of the same window - would resolve this
properly. Not done here, per the decision to close out this phase of the
project rather than keep extending it; noted as the correct follow-up
if this specific question is revisited.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
EXTENSION (2026-08-04): 1400W and 1600W added - anomaly resolved
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Extended the sweep to 1400W and 1600W (4 more runs: 2 powers x 2 modes,
same isolation-verified generation process, same 15%-depth window) to test
whether the 1200W "dip" was a real reversal or the measurement-window
truncation artifact identified earlier. Full 7-point result:

Power(W)  Peak T arc(K)  Transient max|dT|(K)  Final max|dT|(K)  %>liquidus
400       2629.4         218.97                149.17            0.001%
600       3367.0         303.21                168.81            0.001%
800       4155.6         363.33                363.33            0.003%
1000      4913.6         490.33                291.36            0.004%
1200      5598.7         431.88                431.88            0.005%
1400      6284.4         521.71                444.05            0.007%
1600      6974.1         578.28                383.36            0.009%

CONFIRMED: the dip is a measurement-window artifact, not a real reversal.
1400W and 1600W both continue the RISING trend well past 1200W's apparent
dip - 521.7K and 578.3K, both higher than 1000W's 490.3K. With the full
7-point range, the fit is now clean LINEAR (R^2=0.9175) and the quadratic
fit no longer meaningfully improves on it (R^2=0.9334, essentially the same
- the earlier "nonlinear/threshold" verdict from the 5-point sweep was
itself partly an artifact of too few points combined with 800W/1200W being
under-captured by the fixed window).

Relative sensitivity stays in the same comparatively stable band across
the full extended range (0.082-0.106, no trend), reinforcing the original
finding: composition-mode sensitivity scales roughly linearly with power
and with overall thermal magnitude, with no evidence of disproportionate
amplification or damping anywhere in 400-1600W.

Melt fraction continues increasing monotonically and smoothly (0.001% to
0.009%) - no threshold/onset behavior visible even out to 1600W, consistent
with the linear (not threshold) verdict.

This closes out the power-sweep investigation with a single, clean,
monotonic, linear relationship across the full tested range - no further
extension needed.
