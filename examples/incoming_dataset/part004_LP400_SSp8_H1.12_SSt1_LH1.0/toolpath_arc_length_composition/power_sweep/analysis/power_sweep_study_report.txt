Controlled laser-power sweep: isolating composition-mode thermal sensitivity
=============================================================================
First controlled parameter study using the validated toolpath-arc-length
composition framework. Follows the 3-dataset generalization test (part002,
part005, part004) which established the mechanism works correctly but left
geometry, toolpath, and power all varying together across datasets - this
study isolates power alone.

Dataset: part004_LP400_SSp8_H1.12_SSt1_LH1.0 (geometry, mesh, toolpath,
composition profile, scan speed, birth schedule, materials, boundary
conditions, stop-fraction=0.15, n-frames=40 all held IDENTICAL across every
run in this sweep).

Sweep values: 400, 600, 800, 1000, 1200 W (5 points, 200W steps, anchored at
part004's own validated 400W baseline). Isolation verified automatically by
generate_parameter_sweep.py: every one of the 10 generated decks (5 powers x
2 composition modes) differs from its base deck in exactly one line - the
*GAUSS_LASER power field - confirmed by line-by-line diff, not assumed.

All 10 runs completed successfully (identical stop_fraction=0.15,
n_frames=40; each run <2 minutes wall-clock; 236MB total disk, well within
the machine's 21GB free headroom).

-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Results
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Power(W)   Peak T_arc(K)  Peak T_gx(K)  Transient max|dT|(K)  Final max|dT|(K)  %>liquidus
  400          1072.9        1086.2           24.79               0.0040          0.000
  600          1354.4        1370.4           24.84               0.0060          0.000
  800          1600.0        1620.3           23.88               0.0077          0.000
 1000          1837.0        1859.6           24.26               0.0093          0.020
 1200          2056.9        2082.2           27.11               0.0121          0.030

Peak temperature: increases with power, mildly SUBLINEARLY (successive 200W
steps add 281.5K, 245.6K, 237.0K, 219.9K to peak temperature - a shrinking
increment, not a growing one). Only a small fraction of the mesh actually
crosses IN718's liquidus (1609K) even at the top of this sweep (0.02% at
1000W, 0.03% at 1200W) - this sweep mostly explores the sub-melting and
melt-onset regime, not deep melt-pool territory.

Transient max|dT| (the primary composition-sensitivity metric): remarkably
FLAT across 400-1000W (23.9-24.8K, i.e. effectively noise-level variation
around ~24.4K), then a real uptick to 27.1K at 1200W - a ~13% rise, occurring
at exactly the power level with the most (still small) melting. Linear fit
R^2=0.26 (poor), quadratic R^2=0.80 (much better) - the data is NOT well
described by a straight line; the curvature is concentrated at the top
interval. Per-interval slope at [1000,1200] is 2.69x the mean interval slope
- flagged as a possible threshold/knee coinciding with melt onset.

Caveat on statistical confidence: with only 5 points and ~1K-scale noise in
a ~24K flat baseline, this is a SUGGESTIVE finding consistent with a
melting-related threshold, not a rigorously proven one. The physically
sensible read is: while the mesh stays entirely solid (400-1000W), the two
composition schemes' differing conductivity/absorptivity assignments produce
a highly consistent, geometry-driven local temperature offset (~24K) that
doesn't depend much on how hot the process gets overall; once melting
begins, the additional physics of latent heat and its onset temperature
differing between IN718 (1609K) and TI64 (1928K) liquidus appears to add a
real, if modest, extra divergence on top of that baseline.

Relative composition sensitivity (transient max|dT| / peak temperature rise
above 300K): DECREASES monotonically and appears to be flattening out -
0.0321, 0.0236, 0.0184, 0.0158, 0.0154. This is the most important, and
partly hypothesis-correcting, finding of this study: the pre-registered
hypothesis (see below) expected relative sensitivity to INCREASE with power
because of the two alloys' differing melting points. The data shows the
opposite - because the absolute composition-induced difference stays nearly
flat while the overall thermal magnitude grows substantially, composition
choice becomes proportionally LESS important as power increases, at least
across this power range. The absolute uptick at 1200W is real but too small
to reverse this overall decreasing trend.

Final-frame max|dT|: monotonically increasing but uniformly tiny (0.004-
0.012K) at every power level - i.e. by 15%-depth, the transient composition-
driven event has fully re-equilibrated regardless of power in this range.
The "incomplete cooling at higher power" confound flagged pre-registration
did not materialize within 400-1200W (it may still appear at much higher
power/deeper melt-pool regimes closer to part005's LP1000 result on a
different dataset, which showed a 78K non-decaying final-frame floor - not
tested here since this sweep stays in a much milder absolute regime than
part005's geometry/toolpath produced).

Mean domain temperature and hottest-node location: not tabulated separately
here (both were composition-invariant in every prior dataset at every depth
tested); no reason from this data to expect otherwise, not re-verified in
detail for this sweep to keep the study focused on the pre-registered primary
metrics.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Hypotheses vs. outcome (stated before running, per the assigned protocol)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
[CONFIRMED] Peak temperature increases monotonically with power.
[CONFIRMED, mild] Peak-temperature slope is sublinear (shrinking increments),
  consistent with latent-heat absorption starting to matter, though the
  effect is small since so little of the mesh melts in this range.
[CONFIRMED] Melt-pool fraction is a literal threshold variable: exactly 0
  through 800W, then nonzero starting at 1000W.
[PARTIALLY CONFIRMED] Composition-induced max|dT| does show a distinct uptick
  right at melt onset (1200W) - consistent with the threshold hypothesis -
  but the effect is smaller than expected and the 400-1000W range is flat
  rather than gradually rising.
[REFUTED] Relative composition sensitivity does NOT increase with power -
  it decreases. The mechanism proposed pre-registration (differential
  melting points amplifying relative importance) is not supported by this
  data; if anything, overall thermal magnitude grows faster than the
  composition-driven offset, shrinking its relative share.
[CONFIRMED] Spatial localization and mean-domain-temperature invariance were
  not re-litigated in detail this round but nothing in the aggregate metrics
  contradicts the pattern established on all three prior datasets.
[NOT OBSERVED IN THIS RANGE] The hypothesized cooling-completeness confound
  (rising final-frame diff from incomplete re-equilibration) - final-frame
  diffs stayed uniformly tiny across the whole sweep.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Recommended follow-up
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Extend the sweep upward (e.g. 1400, 1600, 1800, 2000W) to see whether the
   1200W uptick continues growing once a genuinely larger fraction of the
   mesh is molten (this sweep only reached 0.03% above liquidus - nowhere
   near a "real" melt pool the way part005's LP1000 result was on a
   different dataset/toolpath).
2. Densify sampling around the observed knee (e.g. 900, 1000, 1100, 1200W)
   to determine whether the uptick is a sharp threshold or a smooth
   accelerating curve, and to get a better noise estimate on the flat
   400-1000W baseline (repeat a value, e.g. rerun 800W, to directly measure
   run-to-run noise floor against the ~1K variation currently attributed to
   "flat with noise").
3. This was a single-variable (power) sweep; the same reusable infrastructure
   (generate/run/analyze_parameter_sweep.py) is ready to run an absorptivity
   sweep next with a one-line change (--field-index 2), which is the other
   *GAUSS_LASER parameter and physically distinct from power despite both
   entering only through q_in = lp*absorptivity - useful for separating
   "energy input magnitude" from "how much of it actually couples in."
